# US BSA/AML Reporting Requirements

Summary written for this project from public US regulatory material (Bank Secrecy Act, FinCEN regulations, FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual). Always confirm details against the primary sources before relying on them.

## Currency Transaction Reports (CTR)

- Banks must file a CTR (FinCEN Form 112) for each transaction in currency of more than $10,000 in a single business day.
- Multiple cash transactions by or on behalf of the same person that total more than $10,000 in one business day must be aggregated.
- The CTR is due within 15 calendar days of the transaction.
- Certain customers can be exempted (for example, other banks, government agencies and some listed companies) through the exemption process.

## Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR)

### Filing thresholds for banks
A bank must file a SAR for transactions it knows, suspects or has reason to suspect involve:
- Insider abuse, at any amount
- Violations aggregating $5,000 or more where a suspect can be identified
- Violations aggregating $25,000 or more regardless of whether a suspect can be identified
- Transactions of $5,000 or more that may involve money laundering, BSA violations, or have no apparent lawful or business purpose

Money services businesses use a lower threshold of $2,000 for most suspicious activity.

### Filing deadlines
- A SAR must be filed no later than 30 calendar days after the date of initial detection of facts that may constitute a basis for filing.
- If no suspect is identified, filing may be delayed by an additional 30 days to identify one, but reporting must not be delayed more than 60 calendar days after initial detection.
- For violations requiring immediate attention, such as ongoing schemes or terrorist financing, the bank should also notify law enforcement by phone, in addition to filing the SAR.

### Continuing activity
For suspicious activity that continues after a SAR is filed, industry practice under FinCEN guidance is to review the activity every 90 days and file a continuing activity SAR within 120 days of the previous filing.

### Confidentiality (no tipping off)
A SAR, and any information that would reveal its existence, is confidential. Banks and their staff must not tell the subject, or anyone involved in the transaction, that a SAR has been filed or is being considered. Unauthorized disclosure can lead to civil and criminal penalties.

### Record retention
A copy of the SAR and its supporting documentation must be kept for five years from the date of filing.

## The SAR narrative

The narrative is the most important part of the SAR for law enforcement. A strong narrative answers the five Ws and how:
- **Who** is conducting the suspicious activity (names, identifiers, relationships)
- **What** instruments or mechanisms were used (cash, wires, checks, crypto)
- **When** the activity took place (dates and frequency)
- **Where** it occurred (branches, jurisdictions, counterparties' locations)
- **Why** the activity is suspicious (how it departs from expected behavior)
- **How** the scheme worked (the pattern, step by step)

Narratives should be factual, chronological and concise, and avoid jargon or internal codes that law enforcement won't understand.

## Customer Due Diligence and beneficial ownership

The BSA/AML program rests on five pillars:
1. A system of internal controls
2. Independent testing
3. A designated BSA/AML compliance officer
4. Ongoing training for relevant personnel
5. Risk-based customer due diligence (CDD), including understanding the nature and purpose of customer relationships and ongoing monitoring

For legal entity customers, banks must identify beneficial owners under two prongs:
- **Ownership prong:** each individual who owns 25% or more of the equity interests
- **Control prong:** one individual with significant responsibility to control or manage the entity (for example, a CEO or managing member)

## Sanctions screening (OFAC)

US persons must not deal with individuals and entities on OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list. Under OFAC's 50 percent rule, an entity owned 50% or more in aggregate by one or more blocked persons is itself treated as blocked, even if it isn't named on the list. Blocked property must be reported to OFAC within 10 business days.
